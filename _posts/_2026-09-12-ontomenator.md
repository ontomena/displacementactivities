# Background 
The Ontomenator maps the idea of 'ontomena', a concept I came up with as a resistance to a whole set of problematic assumptions I saw operating in orthodox, academic philosophy back in the 1990s. Intially, it was a kind of joke based on a notion of materialism with morals, a collapse of the habitual separation of matter and mind. Later, I began to see it as a viable option, at least for my own version of folk philosophy which purposfully lacked rigour and logic, shunning rationality wherever possible. It stayed there in the background for many years. It is only recently, following my Arts Council DYCP grant, that I have picked it up again. 

My first motivation was to try and make some kind of sense out of a lifetime of changing my mind, or better put as adaptation to rapidly shifting transformations in technology, geopolitics and economics squeezed through a wobbling conviction that it was somehow an art practice. Was there any pattern to it? My first passion was music which survived despite a dismal early 'education', up until the age of 16. While music and 'sound', has always been central, it has had to jostle with other forms, in particular writing. Alongside this, I have had a need for academic recognition at various junctures, and this has taken over more or less completely from time to time. I'm over that now. In more than passing respect for Talking Heads' *Stop Making Sense*, I have been reluctant to make sense out of what has been a fairly diverse set of practices over the years. There had to be a way of making sense without making sense. The Ontomenator is my latest attempt at doing that.

During the DYCP (2025-26), I developed my woefully neglected coding skills and managed to construct the map's basic template and initial set of connections with Anthropic's Claude/AI. It has been very interesting discussing such an unlikely array of connections with the All-knowing Entity that 'can make mistakes'. Gosh, yes, can't it just! 

# What exactly is the Ontomenator
Above all, this is a work in progress (or regress!). Much of the content is provisional and incomplete. I will be editing it for as long as I am able, I expect.

It is a series of 41 'Nodes', and each main node may have sub-node/s, or 'Folded Aspects', as they are termed here. There is no limit to the number folded aspects a nde may have, some still have none.

The nodes are arranged in a 'Route', and this can be shuffled or re-arranged according to the user's choice. A fundamental notion guiding the nodes and the route is that everything is equal, it is a flat hierarchy. As in life, we might choose where we go, but when we've been there, there will be a where-we-went. To almost all intents and purposes our route will be linear in time/space single we are single entities. We may avoid nodes and re-visit others, that is the way with the Ontomenator.

The route can be viewed as a 'Presentation', which is similar to a full-screen slideshow. The spacebar and arrows may be used to navigate. There is a keyboard shortcuts menu included in the main map. 
# How do I access it
Firstly, it is designed to be used on a Desktop device in the browser of your choice. The importance of the Desktop mode is so that you can see the full array with all the connections and be able to zoom in and out. It works on mobile, but is best seen as a list (Route order), because the map is a bit unweildy for small screen.  

[HERE](https://www.displacementactivities.org/ontomenator/) it is!

# Can I make my own
Yes there is a blank template with basic instructions on constructing your own. There is no limit to how many nodes and aspects. You can run it in any browser and it doesn't have to be online. You will need a separate folder for media etc.
Here is the blank template. Any questions, you can get in touch via the website contact form. I don't take any responsibility for any issues that may arise from using it, but there isn't anything to worry about.
